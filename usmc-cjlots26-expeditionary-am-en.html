A missing strainer at an exercise,
and a resupply cut from weeks to hours
July 2026 · Pohang, South Korea · CJLOTS 26
27 October 2026
Pohang · South Korea
During CJLOTS 26 (Combined Joint Logistics Over-the-Shore), a US–ROK exercise, a US Coast Guard rigid-hull inflatable boat (RHIB) needed a new strainer — and the part was backordered. Normal shipping would have taken two to three weeks.

Marines with 3rd Maintenance Battalion, 3rd Marine Logistics Group, reverse-engineered the strainer inside an Expeditionary Fabrication Facility (X-FAB), adjusted the design and 3D printed it in under half a day, at about $12 per part — the same as the original. The story was released by the US military on DVIDS in July 2026.

“In this case, we cut a potential two- to three-week shipping delay for a replacement part down to just a few hours.”

Chief Warrant Officer 2 Jonathan B. Salas · Utilities Officer, Regional Maintenance Operations Company North, 3rd Maintenance Battalion (DVIDS)
Defense logisticsExpeditionary manufacturingX-FABSpares at point of needKorea

The problem

One part, one boat out of action

RHIBs carry the Coast Guard’s quick-reaction and patrol missions in the exercise area, and each is valued at around $600,000. When a small part like a strainer is out of stock, the whole boat waits on resupply.

Salas described a demand to reduce supply deficiencies and backorders so repairs can move faster: “Advanced manufacturing is one answer to solve this problem set.”

The fix

Under half a day, first time right

Inside the X-FAB, the team reverse-engineered the original strainer, modified the design, printed it and fitted it back on the boat for testing.

“The first prototype was a success; they were taken aback by the fact that a part such as their strainer can be reverse-engineered and 3D-printed in full in less than half a day.”

Cpl. Kenzie Gallang · Metal worker, 3rd Maintenance Battalion (DVIDS)

Salas said the replacement part cost about $12 to print, the same as the original, and that a cheaper filament could cut another $4 without sacrificing performance.

X-FAB

A small factory taken to the field

X-FAB, the Expeditionary Fabrication Facility, is a self-contained fabrication lab the Marine Corps began evaluating in 2017: a 20-by-20-foot shelter that, at evaluation, held four 3D printers, CAD software and a 3D scanner, and that four Marines could set up on site in two to three hours.

3D Printing Industry reports that the X-FAB system includes Markforged Mark Two printers. The military’s public reports do not say which machine printed this strainer.

Why it matters

Building the part where it is needed

Col. Robert Fairley, commanding officer of Combat Logistics Regiment 35, said the unit is demonstrating that nontraditional manufacturing methods, even in an expeditionary environment, can produce parts that matter to the mission.

Salas added that the printed part kept the Coast Guard’s boats in use for quick-reaction-force and patrol missions, securing the waters around CJLOTS.

Precedent

Markforged at sea and in the field

Shipboard and forward environments are an established setting for Markforged industrial printers.
