Automotive: Performance Critical

When the line cannot stop, tools and parts have to hold up

Graham Rahal Performance (U.S., supercar fabrication | FX10) A fabricator used to spend 20 to 40 hours hand-building jigs, fixtures and exhaust components. With the FX10, the same fixtures are designed in CAD, printed overnight and used the next day. For an X-pipe on the 1,000-horsepower hybrid Lamborghini Revuelto, GRP printed the flanges in 316L stainless steel on the FX10: weld-ready, with almost no post-processing. Read the original →

Subaru of Indiana Automotive (U.S., vehicle assembly) Several models run down the same line, and their tools differ by millimeters. Color-coded jigs printed in ABS cracked under daily use; reprinted in Onyx GF, each model variant got its own color, so operators grab the right tool on the first pass. The placement jigs for bumper QR tracking labels hold a 0.1 mm tolerance. Read the original →

Dana Incorporated (global drivetrain and power systems supplier | X7) Dana runs Markforged printers at sites in seven countries. Its engineering center in Rovereto, Italy printed composite workholding fixtures on an X7; after more than a year in use without failure, each fixture cost 70% less and took 90% less lead time. Onyx GF: color you can read across the floor

Six colors, Onyx-level strength, and room for continuous fiber reinforcement

Onyx GF is a chopped-glass-fiber-filled nylon that keeps the surface finish, dimensional accuracy and print reliability of Onyx, and is non-conductive. It comes in six colors: red, yellow, green, blue, gray and white, so fixtures and parts are told apart by color with no extra labeling step. For higher loads, it can be reinforced with continuous fiber on the FX10. Heat deflection temperature is 138 °C (1.8 MPa). The color-coded jigs at Subaru of Indiana above are printed in Onyx GF.

Aerospace: Mission Critical

Before it flies, a part has to pass every check

Plane Support × McDermott Aviation (Australia, helicopter engineering and maintenance | FX10) Plane Support built an aerospace additive manufacturing lab around the FX10 and printed cabin air-conditioning ducting for the Bell 214B in Onyx FR-A, a flame-retardant material with lot-level traceability. The Civil Aviation Safety Authority (CASA) approved manufacturing the part this way. Partner McDermott Aviation operates the world's largest Bell 214B fleet, used mainly for aerial firefighting. Cabin Management Solutions (U.S., cabin systems for private and business jets | X7) CMS maintains, upgrades and replaces cabin management and in-flight entertainment systems as a quick-turn alternative to long lead times and high costs. Founded in 2019, it bought two X7 printers and prints end-use parts such as in-flight entertainment retrofits in Onyx FR, rated UL 94 V-0, meeting Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) requirements. Read the original →

Hangar One Avionics (U.S., avionics maintenance and installation) Hangar One replaces aluminum parts with carbon-reinforced composites to take weight out of helicopters, and uses Markforged Blacksmith inspection software to measure parts while they print. Manual inspection used to take 30 to 45 minutes per part; it now saves about 30 minutes per part on average. Read the original →

Defense: Mission Critical

When supply lines stretch, making parts on site is readiness

U.S. Army, Fort Irwin National Training Center A hatch plug that helps drivers see at night was out of production; reproducing it would cost about $10,000 with a three-month lead time. The regiment printed one in Onyx for $230 and a 17-4PH stainless steel version for $800. This one application saved the regiment $244,000. Read the original →

U.S. Air Force, Cannon Air Force Base, 27th Special Operations Support Squadron (X7) Night vision goggle mounts break in training, and a replacement costs $100 to several hundred dollars with weeks of shipping. The squadron printed replacements on its X7, with built-in spacers for different helmets, for under $5 each in a few hours; the specialist in charge says they are as strong as the commercial aluminum mounts. Why FX10

Metal and continuous carbon fiber on one machine, right on the factory floor

What the nine cases share is that parts get made where they are used, without waiting on an outside shop or an OEM back order. FX10 puts composite and metal processes on one machine, so this becomes routine work on the line rather than a special project.

TWO PROCESSES, ONE MACHINEContinuous-fiber-reinforced composites day to day; swap in the Metal Kit print engine to print 316L, 17-4PH stainless and H13 tool steel (metal parts finish in Wash-1 and Sinter-2)
MEASURES WHILE IT PRINTSA printhead-mounted laser micrometer scans parts during printing for dimensional verification in Inspection, and measures precision grooves in the bed to auto-calibrate
LOW-TOUCH OPERATIONAutomatic leveling and calibration, four sealed spool bays with automatic changeover; simple for engineers and operators alike
BUILT FOR THE FLOORChamber heated to 60 °C, precision-ground aluminum vacuum bed, single-phase wall power

Composite materials include Onyx, Onyx FR / FR-A, Onyx ESD and Onyx GF, with continuous carbon fiber reinforcement. If you need both composite and metal parts, see the FX10 Sinter-2 Bundle.
