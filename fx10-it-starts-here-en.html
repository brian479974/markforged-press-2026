Automotive: Performance Critical

When the line can't stop, the tooling can't fail. A supercar fabricator, a vehicle assembly plant and a global drivetrain supplier now print their own fixtures.

Official film · Automotive, Performance Critical | about 21 seconds

Graham Rahal Performance: from 20–40 hours of hand fabrication to fixtures printed overnight

Supercar fabrication · U.S.

A fabricator used to spend 20 to 40 hours cutting, shaping and welding jigs, fixtures and exhaust components. With the FX10, they are designed in CAD, printed overnight and used the next day. For an X-pipe on the 1,000-horsepower hybrid Lamborghini Revuelto, GRP printed the flanges in 316L stainless steel on the FX10: weld-ready, with almost no post-processing.

Several models share one line and their tools differ by millimeters. Reprinted in Onyx GF, each model variant got its own color, so operators grab the right tool on the first pass; the bumper QR-label placement jigs hold a 0.1 mm tolerance.

Plane Support: helicopter ducting printed on the FX10, approved by CASA

Helicopter engineering and MRO · Australia

Plane Support built an aerospace additive lab around the FX10 and printed Bell 214B cabin air-conditioning ducting in Onyx FR-A, a flame-retardant material with lot-level traceability. The Civil Aviation Safety Authority (CASA) approved manufacturing the part this way. Partner McDermott Aviation operates the world's largest Bell 214B fleet, used mainly for aerial firefighting.

Defense: Mission Critical

When supply lines stretch, making parts is readiness. An out-of-production hatch plug, night vision mounts broken in training, grip aids for veterans: all printed in-house.

U.S. Army, Fort Irwin: an out-of-production hatch plug, printed back into service

National Training Center · U.S. Army

A hatch plug that helps drivers see at night was out of production; reproducing it would cost about $10,000 with a three-month lead time. The regiment printed one in Onyx for $230 and a 17-4PH stainless steel version for $800. This one application saved the regiment $244,000.

U.S. Air Force, Cannon AFB: night vision mounts printed in hours

27th Special Operations Support Squadron · U.S. Air Force

Night vision goggle mounts break in training, and a replacement costs $100 to several hundred dollars with weeks of shipping. The squadron printed replacements on its X7, with spacers for different helmets, for under $5 each in a few hours; the specialist in charge says they are as strong as the commercial aluminum mounts.
