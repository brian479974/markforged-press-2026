Customer Story · Defense · United Kingdom
US forces send the part files,
British troops print them on site
Mark3D video · November 2022 · British Army REME
29 October 2026
United Kingdom · Defense maintenance & sustainment
During Project Convergence 22 (PC22), Major Shand of the Royal Electrical and Mechanical Engineers (REME), the corps that maintains British Army equipment, explained on camera what his team was doing. The video was published on YouTube by Mark3D, a Markforged reseller in Europe, under the title "British Army is utilizing Markforged", and its description links to Mark3D's customer story on mobile printing with the Mark Two.

To him, Major Shand says, PC22 is about ensuring that advanced manufacturing can link the sustainment elements of the UK, US and Australian forces.

"The benefit to maintainers in the battlefield is huge. We will be able to produce parts forward on the battlefield and return warfighting equipment to the warfighters in hours rather than days. And effectively what we'll do is we'll allow the warfighters to win the first fight."

Major Shand · Royal Electrical and Mechanical Engineers (REME), British Army (from the video published by Mark3D)

Result

Spares US forces could not get, printed by the British

"Our key success has been the ability to receive designs and files from the United States sustainment forces and convert them to a suitable UK file format and print parts," says Major Shand, "so that the US can send us files for spare parts that they can't get through their own supply chains. We can print them and provide them to the United States for use on the battlefield."

Video: Video published by Mark3D, British Army is utilizing Markforged | Innovation is helping warfighters win the first fight (1:45, English audio). The closing card reads © UK MOD Crown Copyright 2022. Watch on YouTube

Why

Repair forward, whatever happens to the supply chain

"It's really important in the future that the REME and equipment support are able to conduct repair forward on the battlefield, and that needs to be regardless of supply chain disruption. So if we lose a supply chain, we need to be able to produce parts forward," he says. "And we also need to be able to link maintainers to a wide network of subject matter experts, which is what we're trying to do here through the use of advanced manufacturing and extended reality."

Equipment

Two polymer printers, carbon-fiber reinforced, able to print on the move

"We've deployed it here with two 3D printers. Both of them are polymer printers. They can print small and high-strength, high-quality nylon parts which are inlaid with carbon fiber to provide that extra strength," says Major Shand. "And they're designed in such a way that they can be deployed in an austere environment, so they can print on the move, or print in any environment that we need them to across the world."

The narration does not name the printer model. On screen, the printing equipment sits in a rugged case marked "FIELDMADE · MOBILE ADDITIVE MANUFACTURING", and the Mark3D customer story linked in the video description covers the Nomad® LW, the mobile printing system Norway's Fieldmade built around the Markforged Mark Two.
